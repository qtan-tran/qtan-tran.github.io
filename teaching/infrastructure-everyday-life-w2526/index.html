Block seminar · Winter 2025–26 · 7, 14, 21 & 28 November

Infrastructure of everyday life

University of Hamburg

Course description

Infrastructures have the greatest impact when they remain hidden, taken for granted, or deliberately obscured. This seminar examines how infrastructures, ranging from food and water systems, health technologies, and urban networks of pipes and wires, to AI regulation, digitalization, and workplace automation, organize modern life while often staying invisible. We will consider how infrastructures make daily routines possible, yet can also introduce risks, unexpected consequences, and demands for constant adjustment.

Students will engage with cases and debates in infrastructure studies to uncover the narratives, meanings, and anticipations embedded in emerging systems. We will discuss how unexpected events challenge routines, and how infrastructures bring together materials, working practices, user expectations, and values in ways that cannot always be foreseen at the design stage. The course also asks what a sustainable and human-centered approach to infrastructuring might involve.

The block seminar takes place on 7, 14, 21, and 28 November, from 10:00 to 15:30. The language of instruction is English; the final paper may be submitted in English or German.
